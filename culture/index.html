Lab culture

Good science comes from people who feel safe to ask questions, try things and fail. These are the values I try to build in my group, and what you can expect if you join us. This is a living document: we revise it together.

Open science

  • Code, pipelines and teaching materials are shared on GitHub from the start, not only when the paper is out.
  • We post preprints, deposit data in public repositories and write reproducible analyses (notebooks, version control, documented workflows).
  • Many of our projects are resources for the community (DEST, DrosOmics, iMKT, PopHumanScan): we build tools that others can use.

Curiosity and creativity

  • There are no silly questions. Bring ideas, even half-formed ones: some of our best projects started as a “what if…?” over coffee.
  • Science is also visual and creative: figures, infographics, apps and drawings are part of how we think and communicate.
  • You are encouraged to develop your own side project and your own scientific voice.

Inclusion and respect

  • Everyone is welcome regardless of gender, origin, background, disability, sexual orientation, age or beliefs.
  • We value different paths into science: from genetics, biology, computer science, mathematics or somewhere else entirely.
  • Disagreement about science is healthy; disrespect towards people is not. Any concern can be raised with me in confidence, and the UAB Equality Observatory offers support on equality and wellbeing.

Mentoring

  • Each person agrees with me on their goals (a thesis, a paper, learning to code, finding the next job) and we review them regularly.
  • We have regular one-to-one meetings and short lab meetings where we share progress and problems, not only results.
  • Authorship is discussed early and openly, and credit is given for all kinds of contributions (data, code, ideas, figures).
  • I support your next step, inside or outside academia, and your participation in courses, conferences and collaborations.

Wellbeing and balance

  • Your health comes first. We do not expect emails or work at night or on weekends.
  • Deadlines are planned ahead, and asking for help early is a strength, not a weakness.
  • Rejections and failed analyses are part of science: we talk about them and learn from them together.

Science with society

  • We share our research beyond papers: citizen science with Melanogaster: Catch The Fly!, workshops in secondary schools and talks for the general public.
  • Teaching and research feed each other: students in our courses work with real data, and some of them join the lab.

Join us

I am always happy to hear from motivated students (BSc and MSc theses, internships) and from people interested in PhD or postdoctoral fellowships. Write to me at marta.coronado@uab.cat telling me what excites you and why.
